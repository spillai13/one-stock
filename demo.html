Why a public experiment

Anyone can explain yesterday's winners. The only honest test is calling them first and showing every result.

6 of 8

Hindsight makes it look easy

Look back at any chart and the winners are obvious. Looking forward, almost nobody can tell. Most strategies that look smart on paper do worse than simply buying the whole market.

100%

Conviction means all in

When the rules find their best bet, they don't spread the money across twenty stocks. All of it goes into the one with the best shot at the most growth in the least time. If a better one shows up, they sell and switch.

Out in the open

How one stock gets picked

Trading strategies compete every trading day. Only a winner gets to pick.

01

Many strategies, one race

Each is a different way of choosing stocks. All of them are measured against the S&P 500.

02

Most lose

Beating the market once is luck. A strategy has to keep winning.

03

Only winners pick

Stop beating the market, and a strategy stops picking. It can happen any day.

04

All in on one

From the winners' picks, it puts everything in one stock and holds it until its rules say sell. No winner? It buys nothing.

The fine print, in plain words
  • No real money is involved yet. Trades are pretend: bought and sold at the day's closing price, by the rules.
  • Results don't include trading costs, which would take roughly off each buy-and-sell.
  • Until at least trades have finished, the page says "too early to tell" instead of guessing. Averages come with a range showing how sure the result is.
  • The research counts are re-checked every day and change; the date they're from is shown next to them. "In the race" means the strategies being tested now, not every one ever tried.
  • "Beat the market" means: in past trades, by more than luck usually explains, on a strict statistical test. The past doesn't promise the future. That's what this experiment checks.
