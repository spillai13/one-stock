How it works

No gut feel, no AI choosing stocks, no editing afterwards. Four steps, the same every morning.

01

Find what's proven

A research system tests trading strategies against the S&P 500. Only the ones whose win over the market is statistically significant make the shortlist.

02

See what they're buying

Of the stocks those proven strategies are buying today, drop anything illiquid, too volatile, or near earnings.

03

Pick one

Same evidence, same edge — so pick the calmest name: least chance of being shaken out by noise. Hold it until something clearly better comes along.

04

Write it down, forever

Every call is committed to an append-only, timestamped log at 10am ET. Wins, losses, and cash days stay on the record.

Why only one?

Most portfolios hold dozens of stocks. That's insurance against not knowing which one is best. Finding the best one is this engine's entire job — so once it has, it puts everything there.

Your best idea, undiluted

If one stock has the strongest evidence behind it today, the second- through tenth-best only pull the average down. Spreading money out buys comfort, not return.

Speed counts

The goal is the most growth in the least time: 10% in 5 days beats 10% in 10. Money parked in slower names is money not compounding.

Nowhere to hide

A basket can bury a bad call. One stock can't — every pick is plainly right or wrong, which is what makes this record worth reading.

The price, paid openly

One stock swings harder than twenty. So every position has a stop set before it's bought, and when nothing clears the bar, the answer is cash.

The fine print, in full
  • Trades are virtual: bought and sold at the day's closing price, by rule. No real money, no real fills.
  • Returns are gross of trading costs (roughly per round trip).
  • Averages carry a 90% confidence interval; with fewer than closed trades the page says so instead of guessing.
